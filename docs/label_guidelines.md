# Label Guidelines

Use these rules to keep manual labeling consistent.

## Composition

- `good`: clear focal point, balanced visual weight, intentional use of space.
- `average`: readable structure, but focal point or balance could be stronger.
- `weak`: unclear focal point, distracting layout, awkward cropping, or poor balance.

## Color Harmony

- `good`: colors feel intentional and support the mood.
- `average`: colors work but lack a clear palette relationship or accent strategy.
- `weak`: colors clash unintentionally or weaken the subject.

## Contrast

- `high`: strong separation between light and dark values.
- `medium`: readable value range without extreme separation.
- `low`: values are too similar, making forms or focal areas unclear.

## Lighting

- `good`: clear light direction or convincing tonal structure.
- `flat`: forms feel under-modeled because light/dark changes are too even.
- `unclear`: lighting direction or source cannot be understood.

## Perspective

- `correct`: perspective supports the subject convincingly.
- `slightly_wrong`: small perspective issues, but the image still reads well.
- `wrong`: perspective errors noticeably harm the image.
- `not_applicable`: abstract, decorative, flat, or non-spatial image.

## Critique Notes

Write one short sentence explaining the most important reason for your labels. This is useful later for LLM critique generation.
